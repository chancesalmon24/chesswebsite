Projectile Motion Quest

Complex Projectile Motion, the review sheet, turned into a game. First you learn to spot what kind of question it is. Then you learn the moves for that kind. Then you solve.

The One Big Idea (read this first)

A projectile is not one motion. It is two motions happening at the same time.

Sideways (x): Nothing pushes or pulls it sideways, so its sideways speed never changes. It just keeps coasting at the speed it started with.

Up and down (y): Gravity pulls it down the whole time. Every second, its downward speed grows by 9.8 m/s.

The two motions do not bother each other. A ball thrown sideways falls at the exact same rate as a ball that is just dropped. The sideways speed only decides where it lands, not when it lands.

Time is the bridge. The sideways trip and the falling trip last the exact same number of seconds. So in almost every problem you solve one side to get the time, then you carry that same time over to the other side. That is the whole game.

Your Toolbox (the only formulas you need)

Set up every problem the same way: up is positive, so ay = -9.8 m/s2 and ax = 0.

SIDEWAYS (speed never changes) vx stays the same the whole flight Δx = vx × t
UP AND DOWN (gravity is in charge) Δy = viyt + ½ ay t2 vfy = viy + ay t vfy2 = viy2 + 2 ay Δy
IF IT IS LAUNCHED AT AN ANGLE, SPLIT IT FIRST vix = vi cos(angle)   this is your vx viy = vi sin(angle)   this is your starting up speed
VELOCITY AT IMPACT (put the two pieces back together) speed = √( vx2 + vfy2 ) angle below horizontal = tan-1( |vfy| ÷ vx )

Why split an angled launch? Because gravity only acts on the up and down piece. The sideways piece does not know gravity exists. So one angled throw becomes two simple problems you already know how to do.

The 5 Moves (every single question on the sheet)

  1. Spot the family. Flat launch or angled launch? Does it land level, lower, or do they just ask about the top?
  2. Split it. If there is an angle, break vi into vix and viy. If it is flat, viy = 0 and vx is the given speed.
  3. Get the time from the vertical side. Falling is what decides how long anything stays in the air.
  4. Carry the time sideways. Δx = vx × t gives you the range, the moat width, the distance. Same t, other side.
  5. If they ask for velocity at impact, rebuild it. vx is unchanged, vfy comes from gravity and time, then combine with the toolbox formula.
